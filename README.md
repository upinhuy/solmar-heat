Solmar Heat is an original top-down 2D open-world crime game set in the fictional city of Solmar. You play Rico Vega, a newcomer running jobs for Mara Solis: explore a living city on foot, by car and by motorcycle, steal cars, dodge or outrun the police as your wanted level climbs, fight rival crews, and work your way up from getaway driver to the man who owns the city. Built by an AI agent for make.fun's "Make GTA 6" bounty.

**This version takes the story to twenty-six missions and $96,200, and polishes the whole city.** Two new jobs before the finale: **three drops across town in a single marked car** — one car, three buyers, 150 seconds against the clock from the market — and a **shootout on the marina pier in the middle of a thunderstorm**, the storm pinned overhead and the helicopter grounded while four of the harbour crew guard a case on the boards under the lightning. The five-star helicopter is reworked too: its searchlight is a real beam now, the pool of light lagging behind a swerving driver, locking on while it holds you and sweeping a circle around your last seen spot when it loses you — and a locked beam vectors the nearest cruisers in a tenth faster. And the traffic lights are smarter: a green wave timed down the avenues, so a driver holding traffic speed meets green after green, and walk signals at every zebra crossing, so pedestrians wait at the kerb while the traffic that would cut the zebra is flowing. The city itself is denser — 56 cars and 66 pedestrians on the streets, 26 browsers in the night market after dark, an estate wagon joining the fleet — and smoother: the renderer skips everything off-screen, the traffic separation runs every other step, and the market crowd starts gathering at golden hour. Press **C** for photo mode and the HUD goes quiet for a clean frame; down by the shore a boat horn rolls off the water every half minute. The story grows to **twenty-six missions** and $96,200.

## The story: twenty-six missions

1. **First Job: Getaway Driver** ($400) — steal the marked car and reach the hideout.
2. **The Pickup** ($600) — meet a contact on foot and deliver a package, quietly.
3. **Hot Delivery** ($900) — a two-star, 90-second dash across town. Finishing it unlocks the Vortex GT sport car at the hideout.
4. **The Tail** ($1,200) — follow the grey sedan: not too close, not too far.
5. **The Solmar Heist** ($2,500) — take the armoured car, lose a three-star heat, bring it home.
6. **The Convoy** ($1,800) — shadow a rival crew's van until it parks, then steal it and deliver it through a two-star chase.
7. **Harbour Showdown** ($2,200) — a marina meet that is, obviously, a trap: four armed rivals who strafe and shoot back. Drop them all and take the cash case.
8. **The Swap** ($2,600) — a marked teal coupe is too hot to keep: steal it and swap it at the marina before the heat finds you.
9. **Night Run** ($2,800) — a pickup on the far side of town, then a 75-second dash to a door that does not wait. At night, against the clock.
10. **Harbour Haul** ($3,000) — the dock crews owe Mara a truck. Steal it from the port yards, shake a three-star harbour patrol, and bring it home.
11. **The Dawn Run** ($3,200) — a black limousine and a passenger who talks too much: pick him up and reach the marina in 100 seconds, with two stars watching.
12. **Rush Hour** ($3,400) — the whole city is on the street at once and the lights are not your friends: steal the red coupe and cross downtown to the drop in 110 seconds, two stars up.
13. **The Shipyard Score** ($3,600) — back to the port: a case of harbour money changes hands at the yards. Three armed guards object. Drop them, take the case, and bring it home through a three-star patrol.
14. **Boardwalk Business** ($3,800) — new turf: a beach crew is moving money on the sand by the pier. Walk in friendly; it will not stay friendly. Drop all three, take the case, and bring it home through a three-star heat.
15. **The Coast Run** ($4,200) — a collector's gold coupe waits on the coast road: steal it and reach the drop at the far end of the beach in 95 seconds, two stars up, the whole shore watching.
16. **The Station Drop** ($4,400) — a courier who will only deal under the north station: meet him on the platform side of Estación Norte, take the silver coupe he leaves running, and have it at the hideout inside 100 seconds, two stars up, the trains rolling over your head.
17. **The Viaduct Ambush** ($4,600) — a crew moving product in the shadow of the west viaduct expects a buyer. It gets Rico instead: three armed men around a cash case under the pillars. Drop them, take the case, and bring it home through a three-star heat.
18. **The Storm Run** ($4,800) — a pilot lands a case at the airfield in the middle of a thunderstorm and will not wait for the weather: the storm rolls in the moment the job starts and only breaks once the case is home. Meet him by the hangar on foot, take the marked sport car he leaves at the kerb, and have it at the hideout inside 110 seconds, two stars up.
19. **The Airlift** ($5,200) — the case is sitting on the runway itself and the crew that owns it is still there: three armed men around it, shooting back. Drop all three, take the case on foot, and bring it home through a three-star heat — while the plane comes in low over the tarmac, on final approach as the fight starts.
20. **The Night Market** ($5,400) — a case changes hands in the night market crowd: meet the contact by the festival stage, under the string lights. It will not stay friendly — three of theirs wait between the stalls. Drop them, take the case, and bring it home through a three-star heat.
21. **Spike Alley** ($5,600) — the blue coupe on the avenue west of the market, and the PD is laying strips tonight: four stars the moment you touch the wheel, roadblocks and spike strips across the route. Swerve around the strips, beat the roadblocks, and reach the drop inside 115 seconds.
22. **The South Station** ($5,800) — the courier rides the elevated line to the far end: meet him under the platform at Estación Sur, take the cream coupe he leaves running, and have it at the hideout inside 105 seconds, two stars up, the trains rolling over your head.
23. **Harbour Lights** ($6,000) — the harbour crew is moving a case along the marina pier tonight, under the festoon lights: four armed men on the boards, shooting back. Drop all four, take the case on foot, and bring it home through a three-star heat — with the boats bobbing below and a boat horn rolling off the water.
24. **Three Drops, One Night** ($6,400) — three buyers, three drops, one night, one car: take the marked car by the market and run all three drops across town inside 150 seconds, two stars up, without mixing the drops up.
25. **The Storm on the Pier** ($6,800) — the harbour crew moves a case along the pier in the middle of a thunderstorm, lightning forking over the marina: four armed men on the boards, the helicopter grounded by the weather. Drop all four, take the case on foot, and bring it home through a three-star heat — while the storm stays pinned overhead until the case is home.
26. **One Last Ride** ($5,000) — steal the Vortex GT from the marina showroom, shake a **five-star** heat — roadblocks below, the helicopter above — and park it at the hideout.

Total story pay: **$96,200**, plus whatever the side work brings in. Progress, cash and records save to your browser automatically.

## Side activities (repeatable)

- **Street races** — drive through the green flag at the north-west intersection: five checkpoints across the city against a par time. Base pay $800 plus a speed bonus.
- **Courier runs** — step up to the depot in the north-east on foot: fetch a package, then drive it to the drop within 80 seconds. Base pay $650 plus a time bonus.

Races won and deliveries made are counted on the pause and ending screens.

## The city

- An 8×8 street grid (2,400 × 2,400 world units) with zebra crossings, dashed centrelines, wheel-worn tracks, manhole covers, textured sidewalks with pavement joints, lamps, trees, palms, dumpsters, benches, hydrants, news boxes, planters and litter bins.
- **The night market quarter** downtown: one block given over to an evening festival — warm festival paving, ten stalls with striped awnings in ten colours and a lantern swaying at every corner, a flower seller and a second-hand book stall beside the food row, paper lanterns strung between the posts that sway and breathe warm light after dark, a festival stage with a red curtain where a three-piece band plays in sets under a MERCADO sign, and string lights sagging between six posts over the plaza. The crowd breathes with the clock: ten browsers by day, twenty-six after dark, strolling between the stalls — and from golden hour onward the crowd starts gathering early — and while the band plays, the crowd drifts to the stage and bobs to the beat. After dark the whole quarter glows — warm pools under the string lights, a brighter one over every stall's lantern, the stage under its own spotlight — and the market's murmur, voices and plates and the band, swells as you reach the plaza and fades as you leave.
- **Thunderstorms**: rain bands come and go, and almost half of the new bands arrive as storms — lightning forks across the sky near you, the screen flashes white with each strike, the thunder rolls after the bolt, the rain falls heavier, the streets darken beyond the night's own dark, and the neon doubles in the wet. About a third of the strikes answer themselves with a second, weaker bolt; the wind gusts inside a band, thickening the fall and throwing pale splash rings on the asphalt; headlight cones stretch further and burn brighter on the wet streets. A storm never stays: it passes, the sky clears, and the next band may be plain rain. The police helicopter will not fly in one — a storm is the one weather that takes the eye out of the sky — and the pursuit cars hold back to 85% of their pace on the wet roads.
- **The airfield** on the north edge: a runway with a painted centreline, threshold bars and the words SOLMAR AIR on the asphalt, amber edge lights that glow after dark, and a hangar with its door open to the street. A taxiway joins the strip to the hangar door, fuel drums stand by the hangar's west wall, a windsock east of the hangar leans with the gusts, and white approach strobes sequence toward the threshold after dark. A prop plane lives there: it taxis down the strip, lifts off, climbs over the northern blocks and circles back to land again — flaring onto the strip instead of dropping — its shadow sliding over the rooftops below, its propeller spinning and its nav lights blinking — red on the left wingtip, green on the right — and its landing light burning longer and brighter while it is low. You hear it before you see it: a chopped drone that swells as it passes overhead, its pitch climbing as it bears down on you. It is on the minimap — the ring widening as it climbs — and it never gets in your way — the airfield lives on its own layer of the city.
- **The elevated railway**: a rounded-rectangle loop of twin steel rails on a concrete beam, carried on pillars down the centrelines of the four avenues around downtown — the whole structure throwing its shadow on the street below. The train is three cars in teal and cream with a yellow stripe and bogies at every joint; it cruises the loop, brakes into its two stations, dwells at the platform and departs, its windows lighting up as darkness falls, a headlight and a night beam on the nose car, red lamps on the tail. You hear it before you see it: a low rumble that swells as it passes overhead. The loop and the train are drawn on the minimap. Cars and pedestrians pass underneath untouched — the railway lives on its own layer of the city.
- **Damage you can see**: every car carries its history. Hits accumulate — first scuff marks along the flanks, then cracks across the glass, then a crumpled nose wedge — and past halfway the engine starts to smoke, a thin dark wisp at first, boiling black by the time the car is nearly a wreck, with orange flickers in the smoke of the worst. Police cruisers wear their chases the same way. And the small street props are breakable now: drive through a **crate, a litter bin or a harbour barrel** and it bursts — wooden and metal chunks spinning away, scraps settling on the ground where the thing stood, for the rest of the run.
- **Traffic signals at all 64 intersections**, phased off a shared twelve-second cycle as a green wave down the avenues — each intersection east or south of the last turns green two seconds later, so a driver holding traffic speed meets green after green. Traffic stops at red, queues, and pulls away on green; the player's car answers to no light. Every zebra crossing carries its own walk signal — a small post on the corner, green while the traffic that would cut that zebra is stopped — and pedestrians wait at the kerb for it before crossing, instead of wandering into the flow. Along the coast road the traffic ambles — drivers ease off where the promenade crowds cross to the beach.
- **Buildings with depth**: every block rises as an extruded mass — a shaded south face pierced by a grid of windows that light up as darkness falls, a parapet roof with vents, crates and water tanks, and shopfronts with striped awnings and neon signs (*Café Luna*, *Bar Palma*, *Hotel Mar*, *Club Vertigo*, *Taller Rico*, *Cine Faro*, *Ramen Puerto* and more) that spill coloured light onto the pavement at night. Glowing **billboards** — *Vortex GT*, *Tacos Rio*, *Disco Faro* — advertise over the street corners.
- **The beach resort district** along the south-west shore: a wide sand beach with two rows of umbrellas, loungers and towels, three kiosks, a volleyball net and two lifeguard towers, its own pier over the swimming water, foam flecks lifting off the shoreline where the waves break — and behind the coast road, four resort blocks: low pastel hotels with neon names of their own (*Hotel Brisa*, *Palms Resort*, *Café Arena*, *Club Marea*), turquoise pools with ladders and lane lines, palms and umbrellas over pale sand aprons. After dark the kiosks burn warm on the sand.
- **The industrial port** in the south-east: four concrete yards with painted bay lines, stacks of corrugated shipping containers in harbour colours (some two high, all solid), gantry cranes — each painted its own colour, rust, steel or harbour grey — with trolleys and hanging hook blocks, and dock crates and barrels — the working end of Solmar, and the scene of the Harbour Haul and the Shipyard Score.
- **A distant skyline** beyond the northern and eastern streets: dark towers shoulder to shoulder, their windows pricking lit as the light fails — the bigger city Solmar is a port of. The towers stand clear of the airfield, so no skyline ever crowds the runway.
- **Park plazas** with fountains that spray and ripple, scattered through the blocks, and annex buildings so no two blocks read the same.
- **The marina** along the south-east shore: a wooden boardwalk with palms and crates, a pier strung with festoon lights that glow warm after dark, bobbing boats (some under sail), mooring buoys, and two-tone animated water with a foaming shoreline and sun sparkles.
- A full day/night cycle with weather: dawn and dusk lay a warm gradient over the view; night brings layered pools of lamplight, neon spill and wet-street reflections, headlight cones on every car and police light-bar glows; and rain bands roll through, streaking the screen and shining the roads.
- Eight car body shapes — sedan, hatchback, estate wagon, pickup, van, the low Vortex GT sport shape, a long **limousine**, and a **motorcycle** with a helmeted rider — in sixteen colours, with shaded bodies, glass reflections, door seams, mirrors, headlights, brake lights that flare under braking and at red lights, and yellow **taxis** with checker stripes and lit roof signs.
- Streets that feel alive: 56 cars and 66 pedestrians at any time. Traffic queues at the lights and yields instead of piling up, follows the street grid around corners, motorcycles weave a little faster than the cars, brake lights flare in the corners as well as the queues, and pedestrians — some in caps — wait at the kerb for the walk signal before crossing at the zebra crossings, and **dive out of the way** when a car bears down on them. Honk the horn (**H**) and watch them scatter — the blast carries half a block now.

## The law

- A five-star wanted system. Crimes — stealing cars, punching or running down pedestrians, shooting — raise the stars; staying out of sight cools them off.
- Police cars chase through the street grid and **aim where you are heading**, not where you are — and they prefer to cut you off from the direction you are driving. Down in the beach district, answering units roll in along the southern streets, so the heat arrives on the coast road itself. Foot cops run you down and hold you for an arrest; a cop far behind sprints to catch up. A cruiser that has been rammed too often trails smoke of its own — the chase wears both sides down.
- At **three stars** the pursuit bares its teeth: an extra car joins the chase and fresh units answer noticeably faster, so shaking a three-star heat is a drive, not a wait.
- At **four stars and above**, the PD parks **roadblocks** across the road ahead of you — two cars sideways, three at five stars, lights flashing, until you get close and they join the chase — and a unit fresh off a roadblock rolls out 12% over its pace for three seconds, so a blown barricade still hurts. At five stars, blow through one and they will throw a **second roadblock** across your road. Ahead of every roadblock the PD lays a **spike strip** across the road: a dark band with steel teeth that takes half a second to unfold, with reflective chevrons painted on the asphalt ahead of it, short enough to swerve around on the kerb — cross a laid strip and your tyres shred, halving your speed and your grip for twelve seconds while the car throws tyre smoke and takes a dent. A chasing cruiser can shred its own tyres on the same strip. Spent strips lie flat on the asphalt, their teeth dulled, until the heat dies. A shredded car can limp to one of two kerbside **repair bays** — Taller Rico on the avenue west of the market, or Muelle 12 on the coast road — and drive over the green bay for a free set of tyres; a bay goes quiet for a while after each repair.
- At **five stars** the PD puts its **helicopter** over the chase: faster than anything you can steal, flying at where you are heading — and now hunting with a real searchlight beam. The pool of light on the ground lags behind a swerving driver, so a hard turn shakes the light; while the beam holds you the lock builds, and a locked beam vectors the nearest cruisers in a tenth faster — the bird is calling the chase in. Lose the light and the lock fades, and the helicopter stops hovering over the chase to sweep a circle around your last seen spot until the beam finds you again. It cannot arrest you and it does not hold the heat open by itself — lose the ground units and the stars still fall — but while it is up, the whole city can watch you run. Unless a thunderstorm is rolling in: then the helicopter stays on the ground, and the sky is yours.

## Controls

- **WASD / arrows** — walk and drive · **Shift** — sprint · **Space** — handbrake
- **Mouse** — aim · **Click** — shoot · **F** — punch · **E** — enter/leave a car · **H / B / J** — horn · **L** — headlights on/off · **C** — photo mode (HUD off)
- **P / Esc** — pause · **R** — fresh run (from pause or an end screen) · **M** — mute

Sound is synthesized live with WebAudio — engine (a third higher on a motorcycle), sirens, the helicopter's rotor wash, the rumble of the elevated train passing overhead, the drone of the prop plane over the airfield, thunder after every lightning strike, shots, crashes, splintering crates, the horn, the market's murmur and the band, tyres shredding on a spike strip, rain on the streets, the slow wash of the sea near the shore, a boat horn rolling off the marina, and chimes — with no audio files.

## Run it

```sh
python3 -m http.server --directory game 8000
```

Then open http://localhost:8000 in a desktop browser. (Or `node serve.ts` for the bundled static server, which answers cache revalidation with ETags and serves a small JSON health check at `/__version`.) No build step, no dependencies, no network: the game is plain static files and ES modules.

`?seed=N` fixes the city layout; `?shot=start|drive|chase|mission|showdown|skyline|rain|port|heli|beach|train|storm|airport|market|spikes|harbour|stormpier&seed=N` stages a deterministic scene for screenshots.
